About / How to use

What it shows. Weekly reported dengue cases in Colombo District in 2026. The grey line is the model with no intervention; the coloured line is the same model with an intervention that cuts transmission by the chosen percentage from the start week, for the chosen number of weeks. Red dots are the cases actually reported in weeks 1–45 of 2026.

How to use it. Move the sliders: the curves and totals update at once. Press Start to replay the year week by week, Pause to stop, and Reset to go back to week 1. Restore fitted values returns all sliders to the calibrated values. A duration that reaches week 52 means the intervention lasts for the rest of the year.

Try this. With a 40% cut, compare a start in week 21 (when cases began to rise) with week 25 and week 29. Then try a short 8-week campaign started in week 5 and in week 25.

The model. A seasonal SEIR model (susceptible, exposed, infectious, recovered) with transmission rate β(t) = β0[1 + a cos(2π(t − φ)/52)]·u(t), where u(t) = 1 − r during the intervention. It is solved with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method using a one-day step, exactly as in the project notebook. Reported cases are ρ × new infections + a constant background b.

Fitted values. The defaults are one posterior sample from the Bayesian (MCMC) calibration to the 2026 data: the sample with the highest posterior density. The incubation period (5.9 days), infectious period (5 days), population (2,375,415), susceptible fraction, reporting rate, initial infections and background are held at their fitted values; only the sliders change.

Limits. One parameter set, no uncertainty bands: the notebook gives credible intervals over 2,000 posterior samples. The model has no explicit mosquitoes; the notebook's host–vector extension shows that some conclusions (for example, that early weak measures can add cases) depend on this choice. This is a teaching tool, not an operational forecast.
